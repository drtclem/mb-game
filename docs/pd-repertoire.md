# Public-Domain Music Used in DCI Shows

Shortlist for MB Game music (decision #9: public domain only). We write and record **our own arrangements** from old public-domain scores (for example, IMSLP scans). We never use a corps' arrangement or a modern edited edition.

**How to read this.** Corps usage comes from Wikipedia repertoire tables (sources at the bottom). Publication and death dates come from a research pass and have **not been verified**; check them before committing to a piece. This is not legal advice.

- **PD worldwide:** published 1930 or earlier (US rule) **and** the composer died 1955 or earlier (life+70 rule).
- **†** marks a composer who died after 1925. A few countries protect works longer than life+70 (Spain and Colombia 80 years, Mexico up to 100), so † pieces may still be protected there. The safest tier is composers who died in 1925 or earlier.

## PD in the US only (avoid for a worldwide release)
- **Stravinsky** (died 1971): Firebird, Rite of Spring, Petrushka. The original versions are PD in the US; the 1945 and 1947 revisions are not.
- **Sibelius:** Finlandia. EU public domain in 2028.
- **Glière:** Russian Sailor's Dance. EU public domain in 2027.
- **Kodály:** Háry János.
- **Lecuona:** Malagueña.

## Traps
- **Every DCI arrangement is the arranger's copyright.** Use the piece, never the chart.
- Arrange from old PD scores; modern critical editions can carry their own copyright.
- Rhapsody in Blue: the familiar orchestration is Grofé's (died 1972).
- Carol of the Bells: Leontovych's "Shchedryk" is PD; the 1936 Wilhousky arrangement and lyrics are not.
